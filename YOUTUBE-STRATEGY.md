## 1. Positioning

> **FHG Athletics helps growing bodies and maturing bodies build joints, tendons, and ligaments that hold up — so you can steward the one body God gave you.**
> Hosted by Alan Hill, CSCS.

### The insight this channel is built on

Youth athletes and adults over 55 are treated by the entire fitness industry as separate universes with separate channels, separate coaches, and separate content.

They are not. **They have the same problem.**

A 14-year-old thrower with growth-plate stress and a 68-year-old with degenerating tendons both need the same thing: connective tissue that can accept load, built progressively and patiently. One is building tissue that hasn't finished forming. The other is defending tissue that's breaking down. Same principles, same programming logic, opposite ends of the curve.

Nobody is saying this publicly. That is the moat.

### Why the faith frame works commercially

"Faithful stewardship of the body God gave you — you only have one" is not decoration. It does three jobs:

1. **Differentiates** in the most saturated content category on the internet.
2. **Unifies** two age groups under one mission rather than two content strategies.
3. **Opens distribution** through churches, which is the highest-leverage monetization rung in this plan.

The line to walk, per Alan: New Testament lessons that a secular viewer recognizes as attributes worth living up to. Not subtle, not in-your-face. Broadly Christian, not denominational.

### Channel identity notes

- Channel name stays **FHG Athletics** (already created).
- Put **"Alan Hill, CSCS"** in the handle area, banner, and first line of the About section. Trust attaches to a person faster than to a logo; the brand name can stay while the face stays visible.
- **CSCS is your single most important credential on screen.** Most competitors in this space have none. It belongs in your About section, your video intros, and your thumbnails where it fits.

## 2. The niche, scored

Scored against the five-axis rubric. Threshold to proceed is 18/25.

| Axis | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Money | 4/5 | 55+ hold the most discretionary income of any demographic; parents of youth athletes spend emotionally; churches have program budgets |
| Pain | 5/5 | Joint, tendon, and ligament pain is urgent, recurring, and directly limits daily life |
| Demand | 5/5 | Both ends of this niche are high-volume, fast-growing search categories |
| Credibility | 5/5 | CSCS, assistant track coach, DIII athlete developed from beginner, 16-week MS outcome, ongoing 70s shoulder client |
| Endurance | 5/5 | It is the actual business and the actual calling |
| **Total** | **24/25** | Proceed |

### Proof assets (use these on camera)

- **Beginner thrower → Division III roster in two years.** Your credibility with parents.
- **MS client, 16 weeks, significant leg strength gain, better able to withstand the disease's effects on her legs.** Your most valuable single asset — strength training for neurological and chronic conditions is a large, desperate, underserved search market and almost nobody serving it holds a CSCS.
- **Woman in her 70s, ongoing, shoulder health and stability.** Your proof for the 55+ core audience.

> **Compliance guardrail:** with MS and any clinical condition, always frame as *what you did and what happened with this client*. Never treatment claims, never cure claims, never medical advice. Say "work with your physician" on camera. This keeps you credible, keeps you out of trouble, and converts better than hype.

## 3. Content architecture

Three pillars. Publish ratio in the first 90 days:

| Pillar | Share | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Maturing bodies (55+)** | 45% | Highest search demand, highest buying power, fastest path to revenue |
| **Growing bodies (youth + parents)** | 35% | Parent-buyer market, fed by your track season footage |
| **Stewardship (faith)** | 20% | The why. Drives loyalty, church partnerships, and differentiation |

Skew topics toward **buyer intent** over viral reach. "Why your shoulder hurts after 60" will get fewer views and more clients than "10 crazy fitness facts." Fewer of the right 200 people beats a million of the wrong ones.

## 5. The monetization ladder

Built on the Cash Code progression, reordered for your situation. **You start near the top, not the bottom.** AdSense is the worst rung and comes last.

| Rung | Offer | Price | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Online 1:1 coaching / remote programming** | $197/mo ($147 founding rate, first 10) | Month 2 |
| 2 | **12-week Foundations transformation** (55+ and special populations) | $1,500–2,500 | Month 3 |
| 3 | **Church program licensing** ← *the big one* | $4,000–8,000/church/year | Month 3–4 |
| 4 | **FHG Certified Coach** training | $500–1,500/coach | Month 8+ |
| 5 | **Digital programming products** (via the sc-coach app) | $49–199 | Month 6+ |
| 6 | AdSense, affiliate, sponsorship | incidental | Whenever it arrives |

### Rung 3 is your business — build it first

**The FHG Stewardship Program.** An 8-week congregational strength program with two tracks (youth athletes, 55+ adults), delivered on-site or hybrid. Includes: programming, materials, training for a volunteer congregation lead, and Alan's kickoff session.

You already have inroads at two churches large enough to want this. The door-opener is a **free 90-minute workshop** — you teach, they see you, the program sells itself from the room.

Eight churches at $6,000 is $48,000/year of recurring, referral-driven revenue that has nothing to do with the algorithm.

### Honest revenue math

You are currently pre-revenue: training is ad hoc, the track stipend is small, the swim club is still in negotiation. That matters for the target.

**Realistic year one:**

| Source | Conservative | Achievable |
|---|---|---|
| Online coaching | 10 clients × $197 × 6 mo = $11,800 | 25 clients × $197 × 8 mo = $39,400 |
| Foundations programs | 4 × $1,800 = $7,200 | 12 × $1,800 = $21,600 |
| Church licensing | 1 × $6,000 | 4 × $6,000 = $24,000 |
| Swim club contract | $8,000 | $20,000 |
| Track stipend | $2,000 | $2,000 |
| **Total** | **~$35,000** | **~$107,000** |

**On the $150–500k target:** $150k is reachable in year one *only* if church licensing lands hard — roughly 8 churches, or 4 churches plus a first certification cohort. **$500k in year one from a standing start is not realistic**, and any plan that promises it is selling you something. $500k is a year-two or year-three number, and it arrives through rungs 3 and 4 — licensing and certification — not through coaching hours, which cap out at the number of hours you have.

The channel's job is to make rungs 1–3 fill themselves. Judge it on that.

## 7. The 90-day plan

### Weeks 1–2 — Foundation

- Channel setup: banner, About section opening with "Alan Hill, CSCS," handle, links
- Record and publish **"For Whose Glory Do You Compete?"** — pin it
- Install and verify the local stack; scope all API keys
- Write the offer: Foundations program scope, pricing, intake form, booking link
- Stand up Brevo and the lead magnet: *The 5 Movements Every Adult Over 55 Should Own* (PDF)

### Weeks 3–6 — First 12 videos

- Two videos per week, batch-recorded four at a time
- Lead with the 55+ pillar (videos 1–8) — highest demand, fastest to revenue
- Every video ends with the same CTA: the free PDF, then the email list
- Draft the church workshop outline

### Weeks 7–10 — Monetize and open the church door

- Launch 1:1 online coaching: $197/mo, first 10 founding clients at $147
- Deliver the free 90-minute workshop at both churches
- Track season begins — document everything. Real athletes, real footage, real results, with releases signed
- Close the swim club contract; price it as a contract, not an hourly rate

### Weeks 11–13 — Scale and review

- 24–30 videos live
- First church pilot signed
- Shorts pipeline running
- **Review metric: qualified conversations started per video published.** Not subscribers.
  - Views but no calls → topics are attracting the wrong tier; move further toward buyer intent
  - No views → the title/thumbnail/hook is the problem, not the niche

## 9. What will kill this

- **Niche-hopping at month two.** The first 30 videos are a data set, not a launch. Commit.
- **Waiting for subscribers before having an offer.** The offer exists from video one.
- **Chasing views instead of clients.** Buyer-intent topics underperform on views and outperform on revenue.
- **Letting the faith frame drift into either extreme.** Too subtle and you lose the differentiator; too heavy and you lose the secular half of the 55+ market. Alan's line — New Testament attributes anyone would respect — is the right one. Hold it.
- **Believing the $500k-in-a-year promise.** Build rung 3. That's where the number actually lives.
